# The Sound of Identity

**Music, geography, generations, and social behavior in global comparison with Bangladesh**

This repository contains a full-length structured integrative review of music as both a force in culture and a reflection of cultural life. It examines geographical identity, rock and pop subcultures, hip-hop and the “gangster” stereotype, metal and alternative scenes, age and generation, peer groups, substance use, protest, war, political violence, memory, and peacebuilding. Bangladesh is the central comparative case.

## Current status

The manuscript is a completed, citation-audited initial academic paper. It is **not yet externally peer reviewed** and reports **no original participant data**. Its empirical sections synthesize published scholarship; its Bangladesh mixed-method study is a future research proposal, not completed fieldwork.

## Central argument

Genres are neither direct causes of conduct nor neutral labels. They are flexible cultural repertoires whose meanings are produced through place, language, history, class, gender, peers, media systems, markets, and institutions. Music can express grievance, coordinate collective feeling, carry propaganda, normalize enemies, preserve testimony, or support reconciliation, but it rarely produces protest, war, violence, or peace independently of organized social conditions.

## Research boundaries

- Structured integrative review, not a database-complete systematic review or meta-analysis
- English-dominant and web-indexed evidence base
- No fabricated interviews, observations, datasets, or results
- No claim that hip-hop listeners are gangsters, rock listeners share one lifestyle, or any genre causes substance use or violence
- AI assistance is disclosed in the manuscript; the human author remains responsible for submission and interpretation
